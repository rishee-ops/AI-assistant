## **PART 1: RESEARCH SUPPORT FOR UNIVERSITY PROFESSORS**

---

### **1. OFFER 1A — Literature Review & Research Support**

**"I Help Tenure-Track Professors at US and European Universities Add 3–5 Peer-Reviewed Papers to Their Annual Output Without Them Having to Spend Extra Hours in the Library — I Handle the Literature Review, White Space Mapping, Journal Identification, and Stylesheet Compliance So They Can Focus on the Science and Writing That Only They Can Do"**

---

### WHAT YOU WILL DELIVER

**1. Comprehensive Literature Review**

- Search and identify all relevant published papers (last 5–10 years) using Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Elicit, and Consensus on the professor's research topic
- Organize findings by theme, methodology, and chronology into a structured, annotated bibliography
- Identify the most-cited papers, landmark studies, and emerging recent work in the field
- Summarise each major paper in 3–5 lines: what was studied, what method was used, what was found, and what gap it left
- Write a synthesized literature review narrative (2,000–5,000 words) that flows cohesively, connects studies, and maps the landscape of existing knowledge
- Flag contradictions, inconsistencies, and areas of debate within existing research
- Deliver in the citation style required by the target journal (APA, MLA, Chicago, Vancouver, or other)

**How This Helps The Professor:** A professor's single biggest time drain in the research process is not the writing — it is the reading. Keeping up with an ever-expanding body of literature across journals, conference proceedings, and pre-print repositories can consume 10–20 hours per paper. Many professors have half-written papers sitting idle because the literature review section is incomplete or outdated. When you deliver a clean, well-synthesized, citation-ready literature review, the professor can jump directly to the section they can only do — the analysis, discussion, and conclusion. One well-executed literature review can unblock 3–4 months of stalled writing work.

---

**2. Research White Space & Gap Analysis**

- Read and synthesize the professor's existing body of published work to understand their intellectual direction
- Map what has already been studied versus what has not been studied in their field
- Identify 3–5 specific research gaps that represent logical next steps for new papers or proposals
- For each gap, briefly explain: why it exists, what studying it would contribute, and which journals might be receptive to a paper on that gap
- Draft a short "Research Opportunity Brief" (1–2 pages) the professor can use to decide their next paper or grant proposal direction

**How This Helps The Professor:** Most professors already know their field well. What they struggle with is carving out time to systematically survey the landscape and identify what is genuinely new and publishable. The pressure to publish in top-tier journals — not just any journal — means the research idea must be original and timely. When you deliver a gap analysis with 5 ready-to-pursue ideas backed by evidence, you give the professor a publication roadmap. That roadmap can last them 12–18 months and is worth far more than its weight in words.

---

**3. Journal Identification & Stylesheet Compliance**

- Identify the top 5–10 peer-reviewed journals in the professor's discipline that match the scope, methodology, and audience of their paper
- For each journal: note the impact factor, acceptance rate, average review timeline, open access policy, and formatting requirements
- Download and apply the exact stylesheet of the target journal to the professor's draft manuscript: margins, font, heading hierarchy, footnotes, citation format, figure labels, table formats, and word count limits
- Check that all references are correctly formatted and complete (no missing volume numbers, DOIs, page ranges)
- Prepare a submission-ready formatted document with the journal's cover page, author information, and abstract requirements met

**How This Helps The Professor:** Every journal has a slightly different stylesheet, and non-compliance is the single most avoidable reason for desk rejection. A paper rejected at the formatting stage wastes 2–4 weeks that could have been spent in actual peer review. Professors often submit to one journal, get rejected, and must reformat the entire manuscript before submitting elsewhere. When you handle stylesheet compliance for multiple target journals upfront, the professor can cycle through journals quickly without reformatting delays. This alone can shave weeks off the publication timeline per paper.

---

**4. Pre-Submission Peer Review Support**

- Read the professor's complete draft manuscript as a critical, knowledgeable peer reviewer
- Check that the argument is logically structured, the methodology is clearly explained, and the conclusions follow from the evidence
- Identify weaknesses reviewers are likely to flag: gaps in the literature review, unclear methodology, unsupported claims, logical leaps
- Write a structured feedback memo with specific, actionable suggestions — not vague comments like "clarify this" but precise guidance like "The section on data collection does not explain the sampling method. Reviewers will ask how participants were selected. Add one paragraph."
- Identify where references are missing and suggest specific papers or sources to add
- Perform a final grammar and clarity check, ensuring technical language is precise and readable

**How This Helps The Professor:** Peer reviewer rejections are demoralising and expensive — they cost weeks of revision time and delay the publication of work the professor has already invested months into. Most professors wish they had a trusted colleague who could give them honest, tough feedback before submission. Colleagues are often too busy. Formal peer review services are expensive. When you provide detailed pre-submission review from the perspective of an informed outside reader, you improve the manuscript's chances of surviving the first round of peer review and reduce the number of major revision cycles.

## **PART 2: GRANT PROPOSAL WRITING**

---

### **2. OFFER 2A — NIH/NSF Grant Proposal Support for University Professors**

**"I Help Professors at US Research Universities Write Grant Proposals That Win NIH and NSF Funding — I Handle the Entire Proposal Structure, Narrative, Budget Justification, and Compliance Formatting So You Can Focus on the Science, Not the Paperwork — If Your Proposal Is Not Submission-Ready Within the Agreed Timeline, I Redo It at No Extra Cost"**

---

### WHAT YOU WILL DELIVER

**1. Funding Agency Research & Alignment Analysis**

- Identify the top 5–10 funding agencies and grant programmes that align with the professor's research focus (NIH, NSF, UKRI, EU Horizon, Wellcome Trust, private foundations)
- Research each agency's current strategic priorities, recently funded projects, and review criteria so the proposal can be written to align with what they are actively funding
- Identify any published guidance from the agency on what makes a strong proposal in this funding cycle
- Shortlist the 2–3 best-fit funding opportunities with a brief explanation of why each is a strong match
- Track open grant cycles and deadlines across multiple agencies so the professor never misses a funding window

**How This Helps The Professor:** The single biggest mistake in grant writing is submitting a technically strong proposal to the wrong funder. Review panels fund projects that align with their strategic priorities — a brilliant proposal on the wrong topic scores poorly regardless of its scientific merit. Most professors do not have time to systematically track funding landscapes across 10+ agencies. When you deliver a curated list of aligned funding opportunities with strategic rationale, the professor can make informed decisions about where to invest proposal-writing effort and maximize their odds of success.

---

**2. Grant Proposal Drafting (Project Summary, Narrative, Specific Aims)**

- Write the Project Summary / Abstract: a clear, jargon-free overview of what the project proposes to do, why it matters, and what the expected outcomes are
- Draft the Specific Aims page: the single most critical page of any NIH proposal — stating the research problem, overall goal, and 2–3 specific, measurable objectives the funding will achieve
- Write the Research Strategy: Significance (why this problem matters), Innovation (what is new about this approach), and Approach (the methodology and timeline for achieving the aims)
- Write the Project Narrative connecting the research to the funder's stated public mission and priorities
- Draft the Evaluation Plan: how success will be measured, what data will be collected, and what the benchmarks for progress are
- Ensure every section follows the funder's formatting guidelines — page limits, font, margins, heading structure

**How This Helps The Professor:** Writing a compelling grant proposal is a skill separate from doing the underlying research. Many excellent researchers lose funding not because their science is weak but because their proposals are poorly structured, too technical for non-specialist reviewers, or fail to make a compelling case for why this project deserves funding now. A well-written proposal tells a story: here is the problem, here is why it has not been solved, here is how we will solve it, and here is why we are the right team. When you handle the writing, the professor's scientific ideas are presented in the most persuasive possible form.

---

**3. Budget Development & Justification**

- Work with the professor to develop a realistic project budget: personnel (salaries, fringe benefits), equipment, supplies, travel, participant costs, indirect costs
- Write a detailed budget justification for every line item — explaining why each cost is necessary and directly tied to project activities
- Ensure the budget complies with the funder's cost principles and allowable expense categories
- Flag any cost categories that require special funder approval and prepare the necessary documentation

**How This Helps The Professor:** Budget sections are where many proposals lose reviewer confidence. A budget that is too lean signals the professor has not thought through what the research actually requires. A budget that is inflated or poorly justified triggers scrutiny and suspicion. A well-constructed budget tells reviewers that the principal investigator understands what the project costs, why it costs that, and how the funds will be used responsibly. This is administrative and technical work that professors rarely enjoy and often delegate — but it directly affects the proposal's evaluation score.

---

**4. Biosketch Preparation & Collaborator Coordination**

- Write or update the professor's biosketch in the required format (NIH Biosketch, NSF Biographical Sketch, or other funder-specific format)
- Draft personal statements that connect the professor's research background to the specific aims of the proposal
- Coordinate with collaborating researchers, institutions, and subcontractors to collect their biographical sketches and letters of support
- Prepare letters of support templates for institutional partners, community organisations, and co-investigators
- Compile all required attachments: facilities, equipment, resource descriptions, IRB/ethics approvals, data management plans

**How This Helps The Professor:** Grant proposals are rarely submitted alone — they involve collaborators, letters from partner organisations, and institutional sign-offs. Coordinating all of these moving pieces is a project management challenge on top of a writing challenge. When deadlines are tight, the coordination work is the part most likely to be rushed or missed. When you take ownership of collecting, organising, and formatting all supporting documents, the professor only needs to review and approve rather than chase down 10 different people themselves.

## **PART 5: CROSS-CULTURAL RESEARCH VALIDATION**

### **5. OFFER 5A — Indian Survey Research & Field Validation for Foreign Professors**

**"I Help US, UK, and European Researchers Expand Their Studies to Indian Populations — I Design the Survey, Collect 100–500 Responses From Verified Indian Participants in 2–3 Weeks, Analyse the Data, and Deliver a Structured Report — So Their Research Has Cross-Cultural Validity and a Stronger Case for Top-Tier Journal Publication"**

---

### WHAT YOU WILL DELIVER

**1. Survey Design & Research Instrument Development**

- Study the professor's existing research papers and theoretical framework to understand what hypotheses need to be tested in an Indian context
- Design a culturally adapted survey instrument (questionnaire) that accurately tests the professor's hypothesis with an Indian population — adjusting terminology, examples, and framing where necessary for cultural accuracy
- Build the survey in a professional tool (Google Forms, SurveyMonkey, or Qualtrics as required)
- Pilot-test the survey with 10–15 participants and refine based on response patterns and feedback
- Prepare an IRB/Ethics Committee application summary for the professor's institution if required for the research protocol

**How This Helps The Professor:** Research findings that hold only in Western populations face growing scrutiny from top-tier journals. Cross-cultural replication is a major gap in social science, psychology, education, public health, and behavioural research — and journals increasingly value studies that include non-Western populations. An Indian sample of 200–500 participants can transform a single-culture study into a cross-cultural comparative paper, dramatically increasing its publishability. The survey design work ensures the instrument is valid for an Indian context, not merely translated — which is what reviewers actually check.

---

**2. Data Collection — 100–500 Verified Indian Respondents**

- Recruit survey participants from relevant Indian communities using WhatsApp/Telegram community groups, LinkedIn, Reddit India, and alumni networks
- Personalise outreach messages for each community segment to maximize response rates
- Implement a two-touch follow-up system (Day 1 initial request, Day 3 personalised follow-up) to achieve 35–40% response rates instead of the typical 15–20%
- Screen responses for quality and validity — removing incomplete, duplicate, or inconsistent submissions
- Deliver clean, verified response data within 2–3 weeks

**How This Helps The Professor:** Collecting primary data from India is operationally impossible for a professor based in Boston or London. They have no networks, no local contacts, no understanding of which communities to approach, and no ability to do personalised follow-up at scale. What would take them 3–4 months of coordination (if they could do it at all) takes you 2–3 weeks using your existing networks and systematic outreach. The professor gets original Indian data that no other researcher in their field has collected — a meaningful competitive advantage in publication.

---

**3. Data Analysis & Research Report**

- Download survey responses and clean the data set — handle missing values, outliers, and response quality issues
- Run descriptive analysis: demographic breakdowns, frequency distributions, response patterns across demographic segments (age, gender, education, urban/rural, region)
- Run comparative analysis against the professor's original Western findings — where do Indian responses align? Where do they diverge significantly?
- Use AI-assisted statistical tools for significance testing and pattern identification — present results that are statistically sound
- Write a structured research report in IMRAD format (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion) covering methodology, sample characteristics, findings, and interpretation
- Highlight implications of the Indian findings for the professor's original theory — what do they confirm? What do they challenge?

**How This Helps The Professor:** Raw survey data is not publishable. The professor needs a methodologically sound, clearly written results section with correctly interpreted statistics. Many professors are confident in their own field's theory but find primary data analysis and write-up time-consuming. When you deliver a complete, draft-ready results section with data visualisations, tables, and narrative interpretation, the professor can review and integrate it into their paper within hours rather than weeks.

---

### WHO YOU SERVE

Your target client is a social science, psychology, education, public health, economics, or behavioural science researcher based in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia whose research has primarily been conducted with Western populations. They are aware that their field is under pressure from journals and funding agencies to demonstrate cross-cultural validity.
